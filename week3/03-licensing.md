# Why I licensed everything under the CC-BY and GPL-3.0

**Date:** 9-15-2026
**Keywords:**
Licensing, Open Source, GNU-public-license, CreativeCommons

A few weeks ago, I licensed everything in this repository under 2 copyleft licenses and this file is to explain the intention.
The two licenses are:

1. [GNU-public-license](../LICENSE)
2. [CC-BY](../LICENSE-CC-BY)

## Why the GPL for software?
I chose the GPL for software for 3 particular reasons:

1. [I am a member of the FSF](https://my.fsf.org/user/164787); I used the alias "kingoftheworld" because the name was available and it's ironic given 
that I try to be as humble as possible (although this can be denoted as a desired view of the self; see [Écrits](https://mudrac.ffzg.hr/~bmikulic/Filoz-Psy2012/JL_0393329259.pdf) by Lacan)
2. Source code should always be available; **This is non-negotiable in the GPL**. 
(See [section 10](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-3.0.en.html#section10) and [preamble](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-3.0.en.html#preamble) of the GPL)with the GPL. CC-BY-SA being the other free license
3. I would hope for other students; either in the future, or my peers in the present; would be able to find use in my works. Ensuring that they can make their own modifications, be able
to share it, and improve the tools is important to the development of technology. However, it is more important that they share the same freedoms with the software that they got.

The GPL is a really strong license that I personally think fits this project well. Student workspaces **should be collaborative**, and open for others to modify and 
use should they decide to share it publicly. Not to say that this is a fact-- but I really do feel like the newer generation of software developers don't really appreciate
the licensing of projects as much anymore. To be fair, our work as developers doesn't really revolve around licensing as much as it used to. Understandably, not a lot of people
want to have to worry about the legality of how their software project works, so they pick a license. However, it is the nuances of how that software is used that makes the key
difference. Our course is about using free license tools to make our careers much easier-- whether that be professional or academic.

## Why the CC-BY?
While the GPL covers the software portions of this repository, the actual writing is not.
While I could have used the GPL to also cover the writing as the data could be considered
*source code*. However, this would not make much sense because the writing is my own-- and
actually accounts for my *real* 'intellectual property'. For this reason, and this reason alone
it would make sense to actually 'copyright' this, but I don't want royalties or someone to have
to deal with litigation for overviewing my work. The best option is to license it with
another copyleft license that is [compatible with the GPL-3, therefore I chose the CC-BY.](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/license-list.html#ccby)

Writing is considered an art, not a utility. The GPL is a technical license first, and the CC-BY is
an art license first. That is why I chose it.
