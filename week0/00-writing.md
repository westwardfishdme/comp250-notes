## Zillow
In response to the Zillow allegations that they were using AI to make housing more appealing to their userbase;
I had found an article from both their [official site](https://www.zillow.com/news/how-zillows-new-ai-mode-works-throughout-the-real-estate-journey/), 
and [WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/backchannel-how-artificial-intelligence-changed-zillow/) that outlined the company's usage and policy for AI.

From the *WIRED* article, Journalist Steven Levy proclaims:
> More recently, Zillow has spent billions aggressively pursuing new technology. One ongoing effort is 
upleveling the presentation of homes for sale.

While investments such as this are typically warranted for development purposes and paying for the labor involved to create such tools and technology;
it is rather only serving the interest of developing wealth for Zillow than to actually appeal to a consumer friendly market. My generation; Gen-Z already
struggles in a toppling job market. A [study conducted by the Ludwig Institute](https://www.lisep.org/content/functional-unemployment-up-for-fourth-consecutive-month-says-ludwig-institute) 
measured what they call "functionally unemployed"; meaning those who are unemployed, involuntarily part-time, and earning a poverty wage account for **24.7%-24.9%** of all Americans. 

Which brings me to the next question:

### Who is this tech for?
As the wealth divide grows between the bourgeoisie and the working class; housing has become a topic of discussion in political spaces.
The average rent in Chicago is currently sitting at **\$2067** a month. The current minimum wage for the city is **\$17.05/hr**.

Let us assume that any given person works a 30 hour week; with a top-end of 40 hours:
```
$17.05/hr * 40hrs/wk = $511.50/wk
$511.5/wk * 4wk/month = $2046

$2067 > $2046
```
From the above calculations, it is evident that the minimum wage cannot even sustain the average rent in the city of Chicago,
let alone what any wage paying less than the city's minimum wage (i.e. working in a suburb neighboring the city, where the pay is IL min. wage ($15/hr)). 

From this alone, it is clear that such technology does not benefit the working class; as the housing market has simply become unaffordable for even those
seeking to rent. So who are the actual *winners* with such technology?

- Zillow; who will receive funding based on their influence with investors.
- AI companies in Silicon Valley who will share profits with Zillow from developing the technology.
- Data Brokers who harvest data from Zillow users and then sell the data to advertising companies; developing
more 'cloud-capital'.

## Returning to our main article...
The article itself spoke about the limitations of AI. As mentioned in our talks about the article; AI is not pushing us to think.
It struggles to conceptualize actual useful information due to the data it's using is scraped from public sources; which are as the article
states:

> "Deep Research tends to draw [its data from] discussions rather than actual published data"

Why scrape from public sources? Well, it's because the information is free; but there is a drawback. 

It understands what essentially is 'fluffed' information and treats them as matters of fact. Therein lies a problem-- if I produce information sourced from a forum chat; 
I can't necessarily claim it to be entirely *true* information, but if an authoritative tone is implied in messaging, **then it is likely the person on the other end
will not try to verify the validity of the statement**, which is what AI does when it synthesizes a response-- it tries to be as "factual" as possible;
to be as "correct" as it can, unless the user informs it otherwise. This is what I believe we mean by "AI is pushing us not to think".

I haven't even begun to mention the devastation to environments with the creation of new data centers; of which are consuming fresh drinking water for cooling and furthering 
damages caused by global warming. Data must be stored somewhere, and a lot of data is being generated by AI; both to create a cycle of information and to be able to create
responses.
